A significant change is on the horizon for CBS News' venerable Sunday public affairs program, "Face the Nation." Mary Hager, the show's senior executive producer and a pivotal figure at CBS News for 35 years, will retire in January. Her departure, coupled with executive producer Anne Hsu's move to a new role within the network, leaves two critical leadership positions open for the program.

Hager's influence at CBS News has been nothing short of profound. She has been the guiding force behind "Face the Nation" for over a decade, navigating it through the tenures of esteemed moderators such as Bob Schieffer, John Dickerson, and Margaret Brennan. Her career is a testament to dedicated political journalism, having covered virtually every presidential campaign, convention, and election night over the past three decades. Since 2022, she also served as CBS News' executive editor for politics, underscoring her deep expertise and commitment to robust political coverage.

Known for her steadfast emphasis on substantive discussions and securing quality newsmaker bookings, Hager actively steered "Face the Nation" away from what she termed the "theatrics" seen in some other Sunday programs. Her aim was always to provide viewers with crucial insights and takeaways, fostering learning rather than confrontational debate. This philosophy was particularly evident during the COVID-19 pandemic, when Hager pivoted the program to become a reliable source of medical information, frequently featuring former FDA Commissioner Scott Gottlieb, who became one of the show's most recurrent non-journalist guests.

Hager's retirement comes at a time when "Face the Nation" is enjoying considerable success. Current season-to-date ratings show the program leading both NBC's "Meet The Press" and ABC's "This Week" in total viewership. Furthermore, it has seen a surge in the highly coveted 26-54 demographic, where it also outperforms its rivals.

While Hager's departure is a planned retirement and not a result of internal pressures or differing visions, it occurs during a period of intense scrutiny and transition for CBS News. Under its new owner, Skydance, the network has experienced other high-profile departures, layoffs, and buyout offers, raising questions about the future direction and stability of the news division. The challenge of filling Hager's shoes, given her extensive experience and deep institutional knowledge, will be considerable in the weeks and months to come.

Social media sentiment surrounding this news has been largely positive, reflecting an appreciation for Hager's long-standing contributions, though some discussions are ongoing regarding the broader changes at CBS News.
